This week we are presenting what we will be doing for our research market. At least that is my understanding, and we arent presenting the actual research. Unfortunately I didn't realize that the announcement was an assignment, and was fortunate I happened to look at the notification. Other than that, the main focus for the first class session was looking at video game markets, which will go well with figuring out my research market since the focus of the assignment is relating the research market back to the video game market. The base of the market, it is clear that video games have a simple set up, where do customers gain access to the product, where do customers spend money for the product, how will we make money on the product, and is there anything else we can sell to the customer for more money. As expected the business side doesn't take into account whether or not the game will be fun, except for figuring out merchendise. and past times the platform mattered a lot, because some games could only run on different consoles, and some games could only work with different devices. Currently though, the market has shown a large shift into making the games accessible on as many platforms as possible, except for Nintendo, and have provided very few exclusives. The other times that we see a necessity in the platform is when a certain type controller is necessary for gameplay. In most cases it is based on whether the game needs touchscreen or a controller. Once the platform is decided, the the next decisions make more sense for figuring out revenue streams, advertising, and allied markets. The next step in the marketing, is if the game is going to be free or not. If the game isn't free then the customer will pay for the game on the respective platform, and that usually would be the end of it. If it is free, then ususally there is a cost to the customer to get the complete game of sorts. Whether that is dealing with advertisements or having to pay to progress in the game, or paying for skins, the customer still foots the bill. Lately it has been shown that these make the most money in the long run, making it so that large game companies will be more focused on keeping the customer spending, because customers will spend more money over time, than a one-time cost. But, lately they have been taking a more dedicated approach which is appearing in both pay-to-play games and free-to-play games. I like to call this approach the battle pass method, due to the most notable being the Fortnite battle pass. This leads customers to spending money on ads (collaborations), Subscription (regular payments to keep having access to the pass), and gambling (loot boxes, skins, or pay-to-win products). Unlike previously, game companies have made it their mission to have every type of revenue stream there is. Ad networks are common in the computer world, and pay-to-play games use them regularly. Currently though, with free-to-play games, we have found that they are focused on using the movie method, or hidden in plain sight. They pay people to play, review, or just use their name to promote the game. Instead of using regular advertising methods, they have found that people like getting what the people they follow get. Which is not new, but a much more accessible method than it used to be. With the involvement of streamers, all they have to do is play the game, and that is free advertising, which leads to more traffic, which leads to more revenue streams. This is the Movie Method because you commonly see this in movies, where they have a product with the logo in the movie, but you don't think about it being an advertisement. And with all of that, after the game is out, they can then see what the customers find popular and enjoy. You see them start making plushies of the most popular characters, or posters of cool designs, just to get people involved in the game outside of their playtime. This one can be seen as Allied Markets, along with getting physical items with definitive editions, any products that are made after popularity is shown. As for the future of the game market, I think it is dependent on new technologies, with the access to those technologies. I believe that the next major milestone to those technologies lies in the VR world. Specifically Full Dive VR. This is the end all be all for video games, the ability to experience the world as if you were actually there is the ultimate version of escapism. But as we have seen with VR already, there is a lack of developers, and a lack of accessibility. Only serious gamers with extra money to spare get VR equipment, and because there aren't a lot of developers, this leads to the fact that the people who use VR, get bored quikly. This leads me to believe that the future is going to be one of two things, technologie driven, or purgatory. Currently we aren't seeing much growth in the game world, but we are seeing a lot of impatience. Games are completely focused on making money, and not focused on being fun. This could lead to the crash of gaming entirely. But it is rare to find someone who doesn't play a game of some sort. Maybe the market will focus more on those that don't think about the fact that they are playing games, like candy crush users. Also with the new AI technologies, we are seeing computer prices go up, which includes consoles and phones. AI is also starting to make games entirely on it's own. AI is causing people to not be able to upgrade their equipment, for lack of income to cost. As of right now it is hard to see the effect AI will ultimately have on the world, with the mass amount of different possibilities.
